## The gap

No road connects one Nunavut community to another. Air service is not a
convenience in this territory — it is the only year-round link between
communities, and the only way most people reach a hospital, a court, a funeral
or a job interview.

That link is provided almost entirely by carriers owned outside Nunavut, or by
regional corporations headquartered elsewhere in the North. Fares are set
elsewhere. Schedules are set elsewhere. When a route is unprofitable, the
decision to thin it is taken elsewhere.

## What a venture here would require

Aviation carries the heaviest entry requirements in the portfolio.

- An Air Operator Certificate from Transport Canada, and the operational control,
  maintenance and safety management systems that certification requires.
- Aircraft suited to gravel strips, short runways and cold-weather operations —
  acquired or leased, either of which is a capital commitment orders of
  magnitude beyond a first venture.
- Certified flight crew and licensed maintenance engineers, in a labour market
  where both are scarce and mobile.
- Ground handling, fuel arrangements and terminal access at every community
  served.

## Why not first

An airline cannot be started small and grown. It begins at scale or it does not
begin, and the certification alone takes years before a single fare is sold.
This sector is a candidate for a later stage, most plausibly through partnership
or acquisition rather than a start-up, and funded by ventures that are already
trading.

## Status

No company has been formed. No certification has been sought, no aircraft
identified, and no route assessed. This page describes a gap NWPH intends to
address, not work that is underway.
