Technical work in Nunavut has largely been delivered remotely, or by contractors
flown in for a specific job. When a system fails between visits, the community
waits.

No company has been formed in this sector yet.

## What it does

Environmental and infrastructure monitoring, and field technical services, under
service agreements with operators working in the territory.

## What a company here would require

- Technical staff resident in the territory — the whole point would be that the
  capability is local, so a fly-in model would defeat it.
- Training pathways, since the labour market will not supply this workforce
  ready-made.
- Equipment and spares held in the territory, because a part ordered south is a
  part that arrives next week at best.

## Why not yet

Less capital-hungry than marine freight or housing, and therefore a plausible
third or fourth company rather than a distant one. What it lacks today is the
trained workforce, which takes longer to build than the company does.

## Employment

Any company here would recruit locally and train in post, on the basis that
waiting for the labour market to supply this workforce is not a strategy.
