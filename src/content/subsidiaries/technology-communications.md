## The gap

Technology work in Nunavut is largely delivered remotely or by contractors flown
in for a specific job. When a system fails between visits, the community waits.
When it is specified, it is usually specified by someone who has not operated
equipment at these temperatures or on these connections.

There is also a growing category of work the territory is well placed to do and
does not do for itself: environmental and infrastructure monitoring, which
requires equipment in remote places and people nearby who can maintain it.

## What a venture here would require

- Technical staff resident in the territory — the whole point of the venture is
  that the capability is local, so a fly-in model would defeat it.
- Training pathways, since the labour market will not supply this workforce
  ready-made.
- Equipment and spares held in the territory, because a part ordered south is a
  part that arrives next week at best.
- Partnerships with the operators of existing infrastructure, whose systems any
  monitoring or connectivity work would have to interoperate with.

## Why not first

Less capital-hungry than aviation, marine freight or housing, and therefore a
plausible second or third venture rather than a distant one. What it lacks today
is the trained workforce, which takes longer to build than the company does.

## Status

No company has been formed. No contracts, partnerships or training programmes
have been established. This page describes a gap NWPH intends to address, not
work that is underway.
